# READY-MADE LOOK: CLAY MODELS ON A TABLETOP
#
# Tactile and slightly funny. Objects have weight and cast real shadows, so
# movement reads clearly — the best of these three for showing a process.
#
# To use it:  copy this file over style_block.txt, then change the palette and
#             the surface if you want a different set.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Handmade plasticine models photographed on a plain warm-grey tabletop, in the
manner of stop-motion animation. Every surface shows thumbprints, tool marks and
tiny lint: it is obviously modelled by hand and is not trying to hide it. Slight
imperfections in the shapes are wanted.

The palette is four clay colours and all four appear in every shot: mustard
yellow, teal, brick red, and bone white, on the warm-grey table.

One soft key light from high on the left and a weaker fill from the right, so
everything casts a definite soft shadow onto the table. Shallow depth of field:
the model is sharp and the far edge of the table falls gently out of focus.
Camera slightly above eye level looking down, static.

Composed for a tall vertical frame: the subject is close and large, filling the
frame from top to bottom, with only a narrow margin around it.

One single continuous image filling the whole frame: not a grid of panels, not a
comic strip, not a storyboard, no dividing lines, no repeated copies of the
subject, no before-and-after side by side.

Nothing enters or leaves the frame and nobody is present: no armature, no rig,
no studio equipment, no set walls, no other objects on the table.

No text of any kind: no words, no letters, no numbers, no labels, no captions,
no signs, no writing on anything, and no markings on dials, faces or instruments
even where an object would normally carry them.

No photographic human hands, arms or bodies at any point. The models move by
themselves.

No dialogue, no music, no on-screen text.
