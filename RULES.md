# Rules

If your video is for a business, or makes claims about anything real, these apply.
They are not style preferences.

## 1. Check your facts before you write the script

Nothing in this folder checks them. The pipeline will illustrate a false statement
as happily as a true one, and produce a confident, well-made video repeating it.

Verify names, dates, numbers and who-did-what against a primary source — the
source itself, not a summary of it. If you cannot verify something, cut it. A
shorter true video beats a longer one you have to defend.

## 2. Nothing that pretends to be something it is not

- **No invented reviews, testimonials, endorsements or customers.**
- **No synthetic likeness or voice of a real person** without their agreement. If
  a real person is mentioned, the pictures should show a **plain, generic,
  faceless figure**, not a likeness.
- **No generated footage passed off as a record of something that happened.**
  A clearly illustrative, obviously-made style helps here: nobody can mistake it
  for evidence.

## 3. Check every frame for invented text

These models write things on objects, unasked. Across a handful of videos they
produced the words "DATA SET A", "DATA SET B", and a **wrong date** on the front
of a machine. Any of those on screen is a false claim, however small.

Step 2 checks the finished frames for this automatically and tells you what it
finds — but it is looking at one frame per shot, so **you are the real check.**

## 4. Say it is AI-generated where the platform asks

Every clip is machine-generated and carries an invisible **SynthID** watermark,
so it is detectable as AI-generated whether or not you label it. Follow whatever
the platform you post to currently requires, and label honestly if in doubt.

## 5. A person watches the whole video before it is published

End to end, with sound, every time.

**This catches what nothing else can.** On one video the sound was twelve decibels
too quiet — perfectly present in the file, invisible to every automatic check
then in place, and found only because a person said "I couldn't hear anything."
There is now a loudness check because of that. There will be another gap like it.

If you are automating this: **you cannot do this step.** Hand the file to a person.

## 6. Check the licence on anything you did not generate here

Everything this produces is generated to order and licensed for commercial use
under your own Google account. If you add stock footage, music or images from
anywhere else, check first: commercial use allowed, attribution required or not,
and whether the licence survives cancelling a subscription. Music and stock
footage are where this goes wrong most often.

## 7. Keep the spending record

`spend.log` is written automatically, one line per charge. Do not delete it. It
is the only record of what a video actually cost.
